Table of values of m*(n)

Table 2 of the paper by Buhler, Golan, Pratt and Wagon (Math. Comp. 90, 2021), extended with new results.

m*(n) is the largest number m such that {1, …, n} can be split into two parts whose power sums are equal for every exponent k = 0, …, m − 1.

Where the new results come from

  • ●

    n = 320, 352, 368, 400, 416, 464:  m* = 9

    Examples of order 9 found by BGPW. Order 10 is ruled out by the paper's Proposition 4.3 (p = 3).

    p320, a second example, found independently by Claude (m = 9): B24B649ED92CD2C36496CB2DB6295D974138BA41

  • ◆●

    n = 336:  m* = 10

    An example of order 10 that BGPW had before Claude's work; it is not in the paper.

    Order 11 is ruled out by the residue condition mod 5 (Claude's proof).

    BGPW's example (m = 10): C87695B43E0ED258AB29C7793D8521DB227C0B746D

    p336, an antisymmetric example found independently by Claude; it is the second part of the 528 example below (m = 9): 9C5479E2669C97071E731A62EBC06F28E87B4961DF

  • ◆

    n = 432:  m* ≥ 11

    An antisymmetric example of order 11 found by Claude. BGPW also showed order 10.

    p432 (m = 11): 5A56B0EE234E8794ADE3D0C384F72A5C63A8BD65035EF015B78B46

    n = 448:  m* = 9

    Claude: "The paper's order-7 example of length 112, doubled twice, p112 # τ2, has order 9. Order 10 is ruled out by the residue condition mod 3."

    p448 = p112 # τ2 (m = 9): A5994DB29B45A0FA5D26B24D665A5A66B24D64BA5F05A2D94DB299A5

  • ◆

    n = 480:  m* ≥ 12

    A symmetric example of order 12 found by Claude.

    p480 (m = 12): B0CBC94F1E2C68D1E3A6371C357C4B1347952BB968236CF6C82CD9CE01F3

  • ◆●

    n = 496:  m* = 9

    An example of order 9 found by BGPW. Order 10 is ruled out by the residue condition mod 27, by the same argument as for 512; the solvers CP-SAT and SCIP agree.

  • ◆

    n = 512:  m* = 9

    The Thue–Morse polynomial τ9 has order 9. Order 10 is ruled out by the residue conditions mod 3, 9 and 27.

    A short proof by Claude, which also covers 496: proof512.txt. The Mathematica script proof512.wl checks its computations.

  • ●

    n = 528:  m* ≥ 10

    BGPW had an example of order 10 before Claude's work. Claude found another independently, by joining an example of length 192 and order 9 to an antisymmetric piece of length 336 and order 9, chosen so that their 9th power sums cancel.

    f = p192 ∨ p336;  p192 (m = 9): C1BE1E21CD63D295A7887A59

  • ◆

    n = 576:  m* ∈ {10, 11, 12}

    The paper's example of length 288 and order 9, doubled, has order 10. Order 13 is ruled out by the residue condition mod 5.

  • ◆●

    n = 224:  m* ≥ 25

    Joint construction: 224 = 3202·112 + 432·48·28, that is, f = (p320 # p320 # p112) ∨ (p432 # p48 # τ8). Hence m*(2k) ≥ k + 1 for every k ≥ 24.

    p112 (m = 7) and p48 (m = 6), from the paper's Table 5: A5994DB29B45A0 C27D8C

  • ◆●

    n = 251:  m* ≥ 53

    With f the 224 construction above, f # f # τ3 has order 25 + 25 + 3 = 53. The paper's 52 is Stong's.

Partitions at powers of 2

Claude: "The partitions of {0, …, 2k − 1} into two parts with equal power sums for exponents 0, …, k − 1: Thue–Morse is the only one for k ≤ 5 (Roth, Siegel and Vardy 1994; Boyd 1997), there are 3 for k = 6 (Boyd 2002), and there are 431 for k = 7, or 367 counting a partition and its reversal once."

Claude: "The count for k = 7 is new, from an exhaustive search by Claude (code and the 431 sets). Of the 431, 303 are antisymmetric, Thue–Morse among them, and the other 128 come in reversed pairs. The same search method shows that the order-7 partition of {0, …, 111} is unique."

The first three blocks are copied from Table 2 of the published paper.

The hex codes follow the paper's Table 5 convention: the left half of the signs (1 = +1, 0 = −1), then the rest by (−1)m symmetry.

The sign # denotes the expanded product, f # g = f(x)·g(xnf), where nf is the length of f. The sign ∨ denotes the join.

Every example whose code is given here was checked by Claude with exact integer power sums.

BGPW's examples at lengths 352, 368, 400, 416, 464 and 496 were not re-checked here.
