Equipowerful Partitions

Table 2 of the paper by Buhler, Golan, Pratt, and Wagon (Math. Comp. 90, 2021), extended with new results.

m*(n) is the largest number m such that {1, …, n} can be split into two parts whose power sums are equal for every exponent k = 0, …, m − 1.

For example, if {1, 2, 3, 4} is partitioned into {1, 4} and {2, 3} then we have these power sums:

  k=0:   10 + 40   =   20 + 30   =   2
  k=1:   11 + 41   =   21 + 31   =   5
  k=2:   12 + 42   = 17   ≠   22 + 32   =   13.

Since the power sums are equal for k = 0 and k = 1 but not for k = 2, and since (exercise for the reader) no other partition of {1, 2, 3, 4} into two parts hits a bigger max k, we say that m*(4) = 2.

(Fun fact: This property of having equal power sums for exponents from 0 up to some k is invariant under translating and scaling. In particular that means it doesn't matter if we use {1, …, n} or {0, …, n − 1} as the set to be partitioned. The m*(n) values are the same. You can in fact start with any set of n equally spaced integers.)

Where the new results come from

Claude: "How the upper bounds are proven. Write a partition of 0, 1, …, n − 1 as signs f0, …, fn−1, +1 on one half and −1 on the other, and let f(x) = f0 + f1x + ⋯ + fn−1xn−1. Order m means that (x − 1)m divides f(x). For a modulus M, the residue sum Dr is the number of +1s minus the number of −1s at the positions i ≡ r (mod M). Reducing f(x) modulo xM − 1 leaves D0 + D1x + ⋯ + DM−1xM−1, which must still be a multiple of (x − 1)m modulo xM − 1. Each Dr also has the parity of its class's size and is at most that size in absolute value. The residue condition mod M rules out order m when an exhaustive search finds no residue sums that meet all of this."

Claude: "The norm bound for a prime p that does not divide n uses the same sums with M = p. Let ζ be a primitive pth root of unity. Then f(ζ) = D0 + D1ζ + ⋯ + Dp−1ζp−1 is not 0: if it were, the Dr would all be equal, hence all 0, as they sum to f(1) = 0; but since p does not divide n, some class has an odd size and so an odd Dr. It is a multiple of (ζ − 1)m, whose norm is pm, so the norm of f(ζ), a positive integer, is at least pm. By the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, that norm is also at most (p ΣDr2 / (p − 1))(p−1)/2. With sr the size of class r and |Dr| ≤ sr, order m needs p2m (p − 1)p−1 ≤ (p Σsr2)p−1."

Claude: "mstar.py and pow2.py repeat the search behind every residue claim each time they run. Certificates that can be checked on their own, like proof512.wl for 512, are still to be done."

Partitions at powers of 2

Claude: "The partitions of {0, …, 2k − 1} into two parts with equal power sums for exponents 0, …, k − 1: Thue–Morse is the only one for k ≤ 5 (Roth, Siegel and Vardy 1994; Boyd 1997), there are 3 for k = 6 (Boyd 2002), and there are 431 for k = 7, or 367 counting a partition and its reversal once."

Claude: "The count for k = 7 is new, from an exhaustive search by Claude (code and the 431 sets). Of the 431, 303 are antisymmetric, Thue–Morse among them, and the other 128 come in reversed pairs. The same search method shows that the order-7 partition of {0, …, 111} is unique."
