Table of values of m*(n)

Table 2 of the paper by Buhler, Golan, Pratt and Wagon (Math. Comp. 90, 2021), extended with new results.

m*(n) is the largest number m such that {1, …, n} can be split into two parts whose power sums are equal for every exponent k = 0, …, m − 1.

    n = 224:  m* ≥ 25

    Joint construction: 224 = 3202·112 + 432·48·28, that is, f = (p320 # p320 # p112) ∨ (p432 # p48 # τ8). Hence m*(2k) ≥ k + 1 for every k ≥ 24.

    p112 (m = 7) and p48 (m = 6), from the paper's Table 5: A5994DB29B45A0 C27D8C

The first three blocks are copied from Table 2 of the published paper.

The hex codes follow the paper's Table 5 convention: the left half of the signs (1 = +1, 0 = −1), then the rest by (−1)m symmetry.

The sign # denotes the expanded product, f # g = f(x)·g(xnf), where nf is the length of f. The sign ∨ denotes the join.

Every example whose code is given here was checked by Claude with exact integer power sums.

Stan's examples at lengths 352, 368, 400, 416, 464 and 496 were not re-checked here.
